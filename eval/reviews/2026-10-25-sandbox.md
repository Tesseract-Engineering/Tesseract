# Spot-check review: sandbox, first full model pass (2026-10-25)

**What was run.** All four sources and the identity pass on the sandbox (bucket `sandbox2`), from an empty bucket:
GitHub, Jira, Slack, Drive, GitHub again with the model off, then each source and identity with
`claude-opus-5-5`, effort medium. Prompts: `thread-v2`, `pull_request-v2`, `issue-v3`, `document-v3`,
`identity-v2`. Answers are recorded under `data/replay/` (git-ignored), so later runs can replay them.

**Checks.** `eval.check_sandbox` 39/39 (coverage, scope, redaction, ACLs, cross-source links, identity
against the roster). `eval.check_extraction` 11/11 (the decisions, systems, owners, customers and
replacements the three stories turn on).

**Volume and cost.** 170 extraction records (GitHub 30, Jira 70, Slack 42, Drive 28): 426k input and
92k output tokens, about 2.5k input per item. Identity asked the model about 3 pairs, twice (4.5k tokens).
Entities: 251 systems, 47 action items, 39 projects, 36 decisions, 27 teams, 21 customers.

Notes below name entities by type and subject, not by quoting the source text.

## What held up

- **Decisions** are the right ones and carry their reasons: the namespaced-claims choice (from the
  #auth-migration thread, SBX-4, the design doc and the rollout plan, with the rejected options as an
  attribute), the CSV endpoint's removal (with its version, date and reasons), ADR-014's drift-triggered
  retraining and its 48h shadow gate, Terraform conventions, rate-limit tiers.
- **Traps read as traps.** The June DRAFT design became a project with status "draft proposal", the
  July copy of ADR-014 a project with status "Proposed"; neither became a decision. The stale customer
  FAQ and the 2025 overview describe systems only, which is what they are.
- **Owners** come through where the owner is on the item: Ishan owns auth-service (CODEOWNERS),
  Karthik owns the retraining work (ADR-014, Drive and repo).
- **Action items** mostly name who took them (standups, the key rotation, the JWKS fixes), per the
  v2 prompts' rule that a request nobody accepted is not one.
- **Noise** stays quiet: 16 #social, #ci and #alerts threads gave 11 entities and no decisions.
  Vague tickets (SBX-17, OPS-16) were not sent; nothing was invented for them.
- **Identity**: with name counts and last-seen dates, the model joined each Jira account whose email
  Atlassian hides to its person (0.85 to 0.88), including the initial-only "D. Pendharkar"; no false merges.

## Misses and problems

1. **People named only in a document's text cannot be owners or assignees.** The model may link only to
   ids it is given, and a Drive document's people are its owner and last editor. In the weekly sync
   notes, four action items carry their owner as an attribute string and no `assigned_to` relation.
   Biggest gap for "who owns X" questions over documents. Fixing it means giving the extractor the people
   the text names (from the identity pass), which adds a dependency between the passes; left for a decision.
2. **A pending choice was recorded as a decision.** The stale "v2 FINAL (1)" sync notes gave a decision
   whose own status attribute says it was pending numbers. The ontology says a decision is settled.
   One case in 36; the attribute keeps it visible.
3. **No `supersedes` relations at all.** ADR-014 replacing the nightly cron appears only in decision
   names ("instead of nightly", "replace nightly cron"), never as the relation the graph could use to
   retire the older design.
4. **Merged Slack bursts mix subjects.** The sandbox's seeding posted a channel's top-level messages
   minutes apart, so bursts join unrelated messages and their extractions inherit that (an action item
   about Terraform sits on the CSV-removal burst). A seeding artifact, not a code fault: real channels
   spread out, and threads are unaffected.
5. **Sheets and Excel files are metadata only** (v1 decision), so facts that live only in "Retrain Runs"
   or "Escalations Log Q4" are not extracted.

## Not reviewed here

Item-by-item recall against every message (the checks test the facts the stories need, not every fact),
and the Bedrock backend (`swarm/model.py`), which has not been run against a real endpoint.
